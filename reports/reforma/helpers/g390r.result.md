# g390r: trim routing and title overhead

Status: COMPLETE: implementation, failing-first regressions and offline proofs.

## Cause and evidence

The demonstrated defect was using the send deadline as the lifetime of the classifier itself. At base `4894add58d7d2903fad15e6ee3ef9a407c1a9812`, `engine/src/engine/auto.rs:12-14` sets both to 2.5 seconds. `:266-287` starts classification even for a direct first send; `:295-330` opens an auxiliary CLI session, marks the request in flight and starts the same deadline. The Codex driver queues the question until initialization and thread opening finish (`engine/src/codex.rs:1924-1934,1965-2000`). The allowed time therefore includes startup, thread creation and inference, rather than just usable routing time. Nothing in the recorded bench prepares a draft: the frozen `g390c2-evidence/final-run/harness/engine/examples/bench.rs:543` sends directly, without `warm` or `route_draft`.

This deadline is below the already-recorded router medians: Luna medium 3.3 seconds and Haiku medium 9.6 seconds, with slowest 9.1 and 28.7 seconds (`docs/product/quota-pacing.md:167-170`). Lower effort had not improved those router medians (`:172`), so router effort and classification quality are retained. Extending the sent-message wait would make first-send latency worse; the fix separates draft lifetime from sending.

Read first: `../bench-1-0/reports/reforma/helpers/g390t.result.md`, cause 3. Also inspected the eight saved final-run result files, benchmark stderr, spawn logs and Faber-owned budget files under `E:/Temp/faber-g390c2-final-evidence/`. A minimal extraction with source paths and SHA-256 hashes is in `g390r-evidence/recorded-spans.json`:

- All eight first spans are Luna, unknown cost, incomplete, lasting 2 or 3 seconds on the ledger's whole-second clock. Each row has exactly one unknown entry and four process starts. Process starts are not model-call counts.
- Everyday example: the Luna span is 1793979706 to 1793979708; the subsequent Sol answer is 1793979709 to 1793979729, known cost 0.049094. Source: `attempt-1-everyday-question-bench/everyday-question/faber/state/budget.json:1`.
- All five project rows have a later Luna span with positive **known** title cost. Title accounting was already present in this capture; this change protects it with an end-to-end regression rather than claiming to recover missing historical spend.

**Measurement limit:** the saved logs have neither timestamped router wire events nor router response bodies. They do not partition startup versus inference, establish that a response arrived after cancellation, or identify a specific protocol failure in those eight historical attempts. The proven root problem is the undersized total lifetime plus cold classification on the send path, reproduced without a model service by a fake CLI returning at 3.2 seconds. No new benchmark or quota-consuming experiment was run to invent the missing timing breakdown.

## Changes, in the requested order

1. `2e1d8b93`: skip routing for one usable model/effort profile. The candidate count now includes availability, subscription quota/billing and the conversation's vendor restriction (`engine/src/engine/auto.rs:70`). Sending returns immediately and dispatch selects the sole profile, preserving the existing no-downgrade rule. The visible reason is `reason.autoSingleProfile`, written in all five locales; the engine returns only keys. Zero profiles retain existing denial/default handling. One vendor with several model/effort profiles still has a real routing choice.
2. `1d3d06f9`: keep native classification off the send path. `engine/src/engine/auto.rs:14` gives draft work 30 seconds; `:235` only reuses a finished, matching draft. With no ready verdict, native sending immediately starts the previous/default choice and explains `reason.autoImmediate`, without opening another classifier request. Injected classifiers retain the old bounded send path. Both ordinary and queued sends pass the conversation identity. Sending clears a queued draft only for that conversation, so completion of an older classifier cannot launch fresh paid classification for text already sent. Already-started draft work may settle its native price normally. No model/effort is changed mid-conversation.
3. Title generation retains the cheapest available, quota-admitted model selection and uses low effort (`engine/src/engine/title.rs:82-91`): Luna for Codex and Haiku for Claude when available; the existing selection handles unavailable or denied models. It retains native pricing, the subscription ledger, the conversation total, manual naming precedence, one-shot generation, output bound and 30-second cancellation. Missing or incomplete native usage remains honestly unknown; no fabricated fixed price or zero is introduced.

The product map, affected design pages and the models page follow the behavior. `docs/next/trim-router.json` has the two user-visible notes in all five languages, as explicitly requested. No new decision, setting, dependency, persistent field, process or service was added.
